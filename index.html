About Smokies Live

A 3D map of the Great Smoky Mountains and its gateway towns, built entirely from free public data and processed into small tiles so it runs in a browser and on a phone.

What you are looking at

  • Satellite imagery: five season mosaics from ESA Sentinel-2 (10 m): winter Feb 2026, spring Apr 2024–25, summer Jun–Aug 2024–26, early fall Oct 7 2026, peak fall Oct 22–30 2026. Terrain shadows baked into the images were reduced using the elevation model.
  • Terrain: USGS 3D Elevation Program, about 10 m across the park.
  • Buildings: 211,501 footprints from Overture Maps (OpenStreetMap and Microsoft). In Gatlinburg, 1,483 heights were measured from USGS LiDAR (2016). Elsewhere heights come from Overture or are estimated from footprint size.
  • LiDAR relief: a 1 m surface model of Gatlinburg from 3DEP point clouds, so roofs, trees and roads show real shape.
  • Roads, trails, rivers, peaks, places: Overture Maps release 2026-11-18.

Hazard tools are illustrations

The flood tool raises water above the nearest river or named creek using a height-above-nearest-drainage model on the terrain. The wildfire tool spreads fire with a minimum-travel-time model driven by slope, wind and fuel read from the imagery. Neither is a forecast. For real emergencies follow the National Weather Service and Sevier County Emergency Management.

Live layers

  • Sky and light: sun and moon positions computed for your local time, so the map shows real daylight, golden hour, dusk and night.
  • Weather and 3D clouds: Open-Meteo current conditions on a grid across the park. Low, mid and high cloud cover become a 3D cloud field at realistic heights, drifting with the measured wind (motion sped up so you can see it).
  • Rain radar: RainViewer, last two hours. Alerts: National Weather Service.
  • Rivers: USGS stream gauges. Fires: NASA FIRMS satellite heat detections, last 24 hours.
  • News and events: WBIR, WVLT, the National Park Service, Gatlinburg events, and Google News (which carries The Mountain Press and other local outlets). Map locations are matched from place names in each story, so they can be approximate.
  • Roads and cameras: park road alerts and webcams from the National Park Service, plus NCDOT incidents. Traffic lines are modeled from typical patterns for the hour and day, not live speeds.

Feeds refresh every few minutes. The live layers need the full site; the sandboxed preview blocks outside data.
